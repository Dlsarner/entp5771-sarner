Selection

Product: PORTALO, a compact manually pressurized portable water sprayer
Campaign URL: https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/portalo/portable-pressurized-water-sprayer
Platform: Kickstarter, Product Design category, creator based in Paris, France
Status as recorded on September 17, 2026: $50,722 pledged against a $6,248 goal, 755 backers, 16 days remaining. All or nothing, campaign closes Thursday, October 1, 2026.
Price: 1x PORTALO early bird at 44 euros, about $51, marked as 25% off an MSRP of 59 euros, about $69. A two pack runs 74 euros, about $86. Estimated delivery November to December 2026.

1. Consumer facing

One person buys this for themselves or their household. The campaign lists its use cases as travel and camping, kids and parenting, personal hygiene, and sports and gear, every one of which is an individual purchase decision made with personal money. There is no institutional buyer, no procurement process, and no one who has to approve the purchase. The 44 euro price point sits comfortably inside the range a person decides on alone. This is as clean a pass on criterion 1 as I could ask for.

2. Describable from the page alone

The product is a bottle you fill with water and pressurize by hand, with a retractable hose and a spray head that lets you direct a jet of water wherever you need it, hands free. The campaign states the specifications plainly: 410 ml capacity, up to 3 bar of manual pressure, about 120 seconds of continuous flow per fill, 205 g empty, and a 60 cm retractable hose with adjustable spray from focused jet to mist. A person who has never seen it can understand what it is, what it does, and what arrives in the box from one paragraph and one image. Nothing about the mechanism requires specialist knowledge.

3. Priced

The campaign names its prices clearly. The single unit early bird tier is 44 euros with free shipping, described as 25% off a stated MSRP of 59 euros, and 356 of 375 units at that tier have been claimed. A two pack early special sits at 74 euros and a second two pack tier at 79 euros, both against an MSRP of 118 euros. Kickstarter displays approximate dollar conversions of about $51 for the single unit and $86 for the two pack. I will use the 44 euro single unit tier, roughly $51, as the campaign price for the Milestone I comparison, since that is the unit a respondent would be valuing.

4. Not yet delivered, and unrecognizable from my description

Nothing has been delivered. The campaign is still funding, closes October 1, 2026, and estimated delivery is November to December 2026, so no Kickstarter backer has received a unit. I want to record two complications honestly rather than let them surface later. First, the risks section states that the product has already been manufactured in a first batch and sold at Concours Lepine in Paris. That is a small European trade fair sale rather than a retail history, and it is invisible to anyone I will survey, but it means the product is not strictly pre production. Second, and more important for the anchor question, the campaign positions PORTALO against portable bidets, and its FAQ directly addresses whether it is "just another squeeze bidet." Portable bidets are a category that carries a price anchor. My neutral description therefore describes the mechanism and the outdoor rinsing use cases rather than the intimate hygiene framing the campaign leads with.

I tested this. I showed the following two sentences to three people outside the class, with no brand name:

"A refillable bottle you fill with water and pump by hand to build up pressure, with a hose that pulls out of the top. It sprays a strong, narrow stream for rinsing off sandy feet, muddy gear, or your hands, with no batteries or plumbing."

Their responses:

"Some kind of camping thing or like for after surfing? A water bottle with a sprayer? No idea what that'd cost, twenty bucks? Fifty? I really don't know."
"Is it for washing your dog? Or like, rinsing gear off? I guess it would be nice to have something other than a water bottle to get sand off of my feet. Huh. I'd have to think about it."
"Is it like for after surfing? I've always wanted one but never knew anything about them."

None of the three retrieved a product category by name, and in particular none of them said bidet, which was the risk I was watching for. The first respondent offered a price only as a range they immediately disclaimed, spanning more than double from bottom to top, which is the behavior of someone estimating rather than recalling. The second gave no price at all and reasoned out loud from the use case. The third is the one I want to flag honestly. "I've always wanted one" implies some vague sense that such a thing exists, but they attached no name and no number to it, so there is no anchor to contaminate a willingness to pay question. I am treating the description as anchor free and proceeding with PORTALO.

5. I can reach the people who would buy it

The campaign's own imagery is a hand rinsing sand off in a parking lot, which is exactly where my sample stands three mornings a week. My primary channel is the adult crowd in the Torrance Beach lot on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday mornings, mostly parents waiting out their kids' surf practice, plus the dawn patrol regulars who surf the same window. My second is the Wedge in Newport on swell weekends, and my third is Luke Wells, former Cal Poly SLO surf team captain, who will post the link to his college team.

I want to be explicit about one thing, because it changed after my channel plan was first approved. The Torrance practice is a high school team, and I am not surveying the team. Most of that roster is under 18, and a minor whose parents buy their gear reports a willingness to pay that is not a purchase decision. I am surveying the adults in the same parking lot at the same hours instead. That is a better sample rather than a weaker one. The parents are the people who actually pay, and it is their cars the sand ends up in. Every channel also opens with an age question that routes anyone under 18 to the end of the survey. Notes for later milestones

Recorded backer count and pledge total on September 17, 2026 so the Milestone I comparison uses a fixed reference. Purchase rhythm is the one weak spot, since this is a durable good bought once rather than a consumable, so quantity per period will need careful handling in Week 6. My reachable population is Southern California adults who end a beach morning loading wet, sandy gear into a car, surfers themselves and the parents of surfers, not a national audience and not the campaign's backer base. I expect my estimated best price to come in below 44 euros for that reason.
