Channels

Three channels, each firing for a different reason. Two are places I physically go. All three exclude respondents under 18, and the instrument opens with an age question that routes anyone under 18 to the end of the survey.

Channel 1. Torrance Beach parking lot, adults. Primary, physical.

Where. The Torrance Beach parking lot during my old surf team's morning practice. About 20 minutes from home in Rancho Palos Verdes.

Who, specifically. The adults in the lot, principally the parents who drop their kids at practice and stay to watch, plus the dawn patrol regulars who surf the same window independently of the team. Not the team itself. The Torrance team is a high school team and most of that roster is under 18, so it is excluded. Minors are a protected population outside this course's exemption, and a minor whose parents buy their gear reports a willingness to pay that is not a purchase decision.

That exclusion turned out to cost me nothing. The parents standing in the lot are a better sample than the kids would have been. They are the ones who actually pay for the gear, they own the car the sandy kid gets loaded into, and their version of the problem recurs three mornings a week whether or not they surf themselves.

When. Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday mornings. Practice runs 6:00 to 8:00 AM. I make my asks between roughly 7:30 and 8:00, as sessions wind down and people are standing around the lot rather than heading in or out.

Roughly how many fitting people. Approximately 20 to 25 adults in the lot per session. Attendance is reliable because practice carries P.E. credit, so the kids have to show and the parents come with them, which makes this the most predictable channel I have.

Access. Public beach and public lot. I asked the head coach specifically whether I could come to practice for this, and he confirmed.

Why this is primary. It runs three mornings a week on a fixed schedule, independent of swell, with a known and stable population. That reliability is worth more than a larger crowd that only materializes on the right conditions.

Channel 2. The Wedge, Newport Beach. Physical, swell dependent.

Where. The Wedge, east end of Balboa Peninsula, Newport Beach. Roughly 40 minutes from home, and I am in Newport most weekends anyway for the family business, so this is not a special trip.

When. Saturday and Sunday mornings, 6:00 AM to 10:00 AM, on weekends with swell.

Roughly how many fitting people. 50 to 60 surfers, bodysurfers and bodyboarders in and around the lineup and lot across that four hour window on a good swell.

Access. Public beach and street parking.

On the crowd composition. The Wedge is a heavy shorebreak and draws bodysurfers and bodyboarders as well as board surfers. Because the product is not board specific, that mix does not cost me. Effectively everyone in the lot is in the market.

Why this is secondary despite being the biggest crowd. It depends on conditions I do not control. When it fires it pays off, and a flat stretch does not threaten the project because Channel 1 runs regardless.

Flat weekend fallback. On weekends with no swell I will work local surf shops and beach adjacent coffee shops instead, aiming to approach roughly 50 people across a morning. I expect this to convert worse than the Wedge does, for two reasons worth recording now. Surf shops are private property, so I need the owner's permission before standing inside asking customers anything, and I will secure that from one or two shops in advance rather than improvising. Coffee shops carry a screening cost the beach does not: at the Wedge effectively everyone in the lot has the problem, whereas in a café most people do not, so a larger share of my approaches will fail the screener and never reach the substantive questions. I am budgeting roughly 15 to 20 completed responses from a 50 person fallback morning, not 50.

Channel 3. Luke Wells, former Cal Poly SLO surf team captain. Borrowed credibility.

The person. Luke Wells, a good friend and the former captain of the Cal Poly San Luis Obispo surf team.

The group. The Cal Poly SLO surf team and its group chat. College roster only.

Rough size. Roughly 50 people reached.

Expected yield. Approximately 10 finishes, not 50. A posted link to a group that size typically returns somewhere in the five to fifteen range even with a vouch, so I am planning on ten and putting the weight on the in person channels.

Status. I have already asked him and he has agreed to post the link and vouch for me.

Method

In person first, digital second. I ask face to face, get a number, and text the link the same day. A posted link converts close to nothing. The same ask made in person and delivered by text converts many times better because the commitment becomes social.

Funnel

For each channel I record approached, started, passed screener, and finished, from Week 5 onward. I expect a large gap between the two in person channels and the forwarded link, and that gap is itself a result about acquisition cost. I expect a second gap between the beach channels and the coffee shop fallback at the screener stage specifically, which will tell me something about how concentrated this problem really is.

Who this sample defines

Southern California adults, 18 and over, who end a beach morning dealing with wet and sandy gear. Surfers, bodysurfers and bodyboarders, and the parents of junior surfers, reached in the South Bay and Orange County, plus one Central Coast college team. My reachable population in Week 6 is set to that group, not to a national audience and not to the campaign's backer base. I will report the surfer and parent segments separately, since they have the same problem from different seats and may not price it the same way.
