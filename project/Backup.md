If PORTALO fails the anchor test, POWASH 3.0 is my replacement, and it clears most of the five criteria for the same reasons PORTALO does.

It is consumer facing. One person buys a cordless cleaning tool for their own car, gear, or dog, with no institutional buyer and no approval process.

It is describable from the page alone. The campaign publishes a full specification table: 3.0 MPa maximum pressure, 1.98 lb, 40 minutes of continuous runtime, 180 L/H flow, a 5 in 1 nozzle with four pressure levels, IP65 water resistance, and three ways to draw water including a bottle adapter and a faucet fitting. A person who has never seen it understands what it does from one image and one paragraph.

It is priced, with three named tiers on the campaign page.

I can reach its buyers through the channels already approved in channels.md. The campaign's own imagery is a surfer rinsing a board and a wetsuit in a parking lot, which is the same situation my Torrance and Wedge samples are standing in.

Three honest problems, recorded now rather than discovered later.

The price is the first. At $125 to $141 this is two and a half times PORTALO's $51, and it pushes hard against the guidance about respondents answering honestly on inexpensive objects. A stated willingness to pay $130 is much less reliable than a stated willingness to pay $50, because fewer respondents have ever actually made a $130 impulse decision on outdoor gear.

The second is that there is no single unit. Every tier bundles the washer with an 8L water bag, and the water bag is separately listed as a $17 add on. So the thing a buyer pictures and the thing the campaign sells are not the same object, and I would have to decide whether my unit is the washer alone or the bundle. PORTALO does not have this problem.

The third is the most serious for criterion 4. This is version 3.0, and the campaign timeline states that the previous POWASH model raised over $300,000 through crowdfunding in Japan in March 2026. Earlier versions of this product have therefore already shipped to real customers. The product is not new to the world in the way PORTALO's is, and a respondent who has encountered the earlier model has a price in their head before I ask. Cordless pressure washer is also a recognizable Amazon category with visible prices, and the campaign's own comparison table positions POWASH against generic cordless and handheld models. I would run the same three person anchor test before switching, and I would expect it to be harder to pass than PORTALO's was.

Second alternative if both fail: ROKEKO, the smallest 5 in 1 cordless high pressure washer, 26 days remaining and 562% funded, surfaced as a similar project on the POWASH page. Same buyer and same channels, but it shares POWASH's category anchor problem, so it is a third choice rather than a second.
