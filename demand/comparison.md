# Comparison with the campaign

## What the campaign shows

- **Tiers:** early bird €44 (about $51), stated MSRP €59 (about $69), and a two-pack at €74 (about $86).
- **Backers:** 755 on 17 September 2026 with 16 days left, from a worldwide audience, raising $50,722 against a $6,248 goal. The campaign closed on 1 October, so the final count is at least 755.

These are not a demand curve. Each tier is a different bundle, the early-bird tier is time- and quantity-limited, and the backers chose themselves by visiting a crowdfunding page for this product. What they support is the comparison below.

## Price

My estimated profit-maximising price, before ordering, is **$55 at low costs and $75 at high costs** (curve.md, section 4). The campaign charges **$51**, with $69 as the stated retail price.

These are closer than I expected. The campaign's $51 sits just under my low-cost estimate, and its $69 MSRP sits between my two. At $51 my curve says 6 of 21 (29%) would buy, and at $69 it says 3 of 21 (14%). My data is consistent with the campaign's prices, but it can't distinguish between them, because the gap from $51 to $69 is three respondents.

## Quantity

At $51, 29% of my respondents would buy. Scaled to my reachable population of 700 to 2,000 adults (profit/scale.md), that is **200 to 570 people willing in principle**, before any discount for the gap between saying and buying. The campaign got **at least 755 actual buyers** in a month.

## Why they diverge

**They reached a different population.** The campaign drew a worldwide audience of people who clicked through to a page about this exact product, and that is selection on the outcome. I reached adults at one beach in Torrance on one morning, most of whom are surfers. Nine of my 21 usually have a working shower where they go, and two already own a rinse kit. A Kickstarter visitor has already decided the problem is worth solving. Most of my respondents haven't.

**Their description sells and mine doesn't.** The campaign leads with uses: personal hygiene (it positions itself against portable bidets), sandy feet, muddy gear. My description was deliberately neutral and led with what it is, "a refillable bottle that sprays water." Several of my respondents read it as a drinking bottle and two didn't understand what it was for. The campaign's framing widens the problem it solves, and mine probably narrowed it.

**A pledge is a purchase and my answer isn't.** That gap usually makes stated willingness overstate real buying, so it should push my numbers above theirs. It hasn't, which tells me the first two reasons are larger than this one.

**What I can't explain away.** On a per-person basis, my sample is less keen than theirs, and on total reach, my channel is far smaller than theirs. The 755 backers do not show that 755 people in Southern California would buy. They show that a worldwide crowdfunding audience, shown a selling description, would. My numbers are the better guide to what happens if I try to sell the remaining 245 units myself, at Torrance, to people who did not come looking for it.
