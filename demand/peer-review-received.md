## The valuation block, checked against the defects named in the assignment

- **Period stated in the question:** yes. Q9 asks for one unit and explains that most people buy one and keep it for a few years.
- **Zero easy to give:** yes. The question says zero is a normal and useful answer. The field accepts 0, has no default and no slider, and requires only a number greater than or equal to 0.
- **Anchors:** not applicable. I use yes/no demand, which asks for a single maximum, so there are no anchors to put in order.
- **Required field trapping zeros:** tested. I entered 0 on the live form and it was accepted.

## What I changed while building and testing

**Switched from Microsoft Forms to Google Forms.** Microsoft Forms would not get past its welcome screen on my university account. Google Forms only branches between sections, so I gave every screening question its own section.

**Dropped the beach, lake or river screener.** The sand, salt, mud and wet gear question already says "after being outdoors," so it screens for the same people. That saved a section and one question.

**Retitled the form.** It was "Outdoor gear and sandy cars," which showed up in the link preview when texted and signalled the answer I wanted to the sand screener that follows. It is now "Outdoor gear survey."

**Used a cleaned campaign photo.** I removed the slogan and a small mark on the bottle so there is nothing to search for. The only clean shot had a Japanese temple in the background rather than a beach. I kept it because showing the real product matters more than the setting, and I recorded in instrument-notes.md that a premium travel setting may nudge valuations up.

**One confirmation message for everyone.** Google Forms cannot show screened-out respondents a different ending, so I wrote one message that works for both groups.

## What I decided not to change

**The four-question appeal block.** It is the longest section and adds nothing to the demand estimate. I kept it because reading the form straight through, the price question feels like haggling without it. My reasoning is in instrument-notes.md.
