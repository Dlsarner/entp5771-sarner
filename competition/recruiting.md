## A fresh sample

None of survey one's 21 respondents can be used, because each has already named a price for PORTALO on its own and would anchor to it. The survey is anonymous, so I can't check against a list. Instead I rely on three things:

1. **Q2 screens them out.** Anyone who did survey one is sent straight to the end.
2. **The coach's email tells them.** It says that if you did my survey in October, you don't need to do this one.
3. **I don't reuse the people I know were in it.** No post to Luke's group (that produced one response, already used) and no asking my sibling and cousin again. At Torrance, I skip the parents I remember surveying on 29 October.

## What the first survey taught me

From demand/funnel.md:

- **One channel worked.** In person at Torrance on 29 October, the coach emailed parents beforehand and people filled it in on their own phones while I stood there: **35 approached, 23 started, 18 finished (51%)**.
- **"Do it later" failed every time.** Torrance in September: 10 approached, 5 took the link, 0 started. Luke's group post: about 50 reached, 1 finished.
- **The screen removed 5 of 23 (22%)**, mostly "someone else pays." Nobody quit partway through.

The three-sentence lesson from the funnel checkpoint: the difference between 0% and 51% wasn't the people, it was whether the ask and the answer happened in the same minute and whether someone they trusted had vouched for me first. So this time every ask is in person, completed on the spot, with the coach's introduction first. I'm not counting on any channel that asks people to do it later.

## The ask

**Coach's email, sent the day before:**

> Hi everyone, a University of Utah business student, Dawson, will be at practice on [DAY] asking parents to do a 6-minute anonymous survey about outdoor gear for a class project. Nothing is being sold. If you already did his survey in October, he doesn't need you again. Thanks!

**In person:**

> Hi, sorry to bother you. I'm the business student [coach] emailed about, doing a short research survey on outdoor gear. It's about six minutes on your phone, anonymous, and I'm not selling anything. Would you be up for doing it now?

If they say yes, they scan a QR code on my phone, or I text them the link if they'd rather, and they do it there. If they say "later," I thank them and move on. Later has never come.

What changed from survey one: "four minutes" is now "six," because the survey is longer and people who are told four and get six quit. "Do it now" replaces "I can text you the link." Neither the products, the brands, nor any prices are mentioned before the form shows them.

## Target, and the arithmetic

**Approaches per finished response in the channel that worked:** 35 ÷ 18 = **1.9**.

**Adjusted for this survey:** it's about 50% longer and adds a repeat-respondent screen, so I assume about 15% more loss than last time. The finish rate falls from 51% to about 0.51 × 0.85 = 44%, which is **2.3 approaches per response**.

**Target: 30 finished responses.** 30 × 2.3 ≈ **70 approaches**, two sessions the size of 29 October.

**Floor: about 15.** If only one session happens, 35 ÷ 2.3 ≈ 15. I'll say so in this file by Sunday 15 November rather than discover it in Week 12.

**Why 30 and not more.** My first collection was thin. Eighteen of my 21 usable responses came from one morning, and I've never run more than one productive session in a two-week window. Thirty needs two good mornings in two weeks. That's achievable, and I'd rather hit 30 than plan for 50 and miss.
