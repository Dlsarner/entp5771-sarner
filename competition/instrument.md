## Section 3. The two products

Below are two products that do a similar job. Please read both before going on. They are labelled A and B, and the letters don't mean anything.

### Product A

[IMAGE: the cleaned product photo from survey one. Product only, slogan and marks removed.]

A refillable bottle that sprays water under pressure. You fill it from any tap, bottle, or jug, then pump the top by hand about ten times to pressurize it. A thin hose pulls out of the top with a small spray head on the end, so you point the spray where you want it while the bottle stays put.

Specifications, as given by the maker:

- **Capacity:** 410 ml, about 14 fl oz, a little less than a standard water bottle
- **Weight:** about 7 oz (205 g) empty, about 1⅓ lb full
- **Pressure:** up to 3 bar, about 44 psi, built by hand pumping before use; no pumping while spraying
- **Spray time:** up to about 2 minutes of continuous spray per fill
- **Hose:** 60 cm, about 2 ft, retracts into the bottle
- **Spray settings:** 2, a narrow jet and a wide mist; the spray head clips magnetically to the bottle when not in use
- **Filling:** from any tap, bottle, or jug
- **Power:** none; no batteries, no charging

What it does not do: it holds one fill at a time, so for bigger jobs you refill and re-pump it repeatedly. It does not heat water. It is not a shower and will not rinse off a whole person on one fill.

In the box: the bottle, the hose, the spray head, and a carry strap.

### Product B

[IMAGE: one plain product photo, logo and wordmark covered, same size and same plain background as Product A's image.]

A hard plastic water tank with a carry handle. You fill it by connecting it to an outdoor tap with a threaded hose fitting, and the tank stores the tap's water pressure as it fills, so it sprays under pressure later without pumping. A hose wraps around the outside of the tank with a spray nozzle on the end.

Specifications, as given by the maker:

- **Capacity:** about 1.5 gallons (5.7 litres), a little under three 2-litre soda bottles
- **Weight:** about 6½ lb empty, about 19 lb full
- **Pressure:** whatever the tap it was filled from supplies, typically 40 to 65 psi (about 3 to 4.5 bar) at a household outdoor tap; holds pressure for up to a month
- **Spray time:** up to about 3 to 4 minutes of continuous spray per fill
- **Hose:** 6 ft (1.8 m), wraps around the tank
- **Spray settings:** 5: jet, mist, center, flat, and shower
- **Filling:** from an outdoor tap with a threaded hose fitting
- **Power:** none; no batteries, no charging

What it does not do: it needs a threaded outdoor tap to fill under pressure; without one, re-pressurizing it takes a hand pump that is sold separately. It does not heat water; a heater is sold separately. It holds one fill at a time, so once it runs out you need a tap again to refill it.

In the box: the tank, the hose with an on/off valve, the spray nozzle, and an adapter for connecting to an outdoor tap.

## What each question is for

| Question | Feeds |
|---|---|
| Q1 | Age gate. Minors are excluded before anything else |
| Q2 | Fresh sample. Keeps survey one's respondents out |
| Q3, Q4 | Screen on the customer's purchase decision, as in survey one |
| Q5 to Q8 | Frames the exchange as help before price; appeal of A against B as a cross-check on the WTP gap |
| Q9, Q10 | Each respondent's two WTP values, which set the price levels for conditions 2 to 4 |
| Q11 to Q14 | A's equation: own-price effect (condition 2 against 1), cross-price effect (3 against 1), both moving (4) |
| Q15 to Q18 | B's equation, the same three comparisons from the rival's side, including how many of A's customers B gains when A's price rises |
| Q19 | Sanity check on the substitution numbers, in the respondent's words |
| Q20 | Segment: surfer against surf parent, as in survey one |
| Q21 | Segment: people who already have a shower or hose where they go |
| Q22 | Famous-rival check: whether people who know B give WTP for B near its shelf price (see instrument-notes.md) |
