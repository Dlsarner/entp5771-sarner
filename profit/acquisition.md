## From responses to customers, in the best channel

A finished survey is not a customer. Of the 18 people who finished on 29 October:

| Price | Willing at that price | Approaches per willing person | Time on site per willing person |
|---|---|---|---|
| $35 | 12 of 18 | 2.9 | about 30 minutes |

These assume every willing person actually buys. Nobody paid anything, so I have no measure of how many would.

## Margin per unit against the effort

| | Low costs | High costs |
|---|---|---|
| Margin per unit at $51 (p − c) | $42.64 | $32.45 |
| Margin per unit at $35 | $27.11 | $17.01 |

**How many units does one customer have to buy?** Exactly one is all they will buy. PORTALO is a durable product, demand is yes/no, and nobody buys a second in the year. So the margin on the first unit has to cover the effort of finding that customer, or nothing ever will.

**If I had to pay for it.** Suppose I paid someone $20 an hour to do what I did on 29 October. That rate is my own round figure, not a measured cost. One willing person at $51 then costs about **$20**, and at $35 about **$10**. If only half of willing people actually buy, those double to $40 and $20.

## Does the margin survive?

**On a single unit, barely.** At $51, a $42.64 margin covers $20 of effort only if at least 47% of willing people actually buy. At high costs ($32.45), at least 62% have to.

**Across the whole commitment, no.** The margin also has to cover f. With effort included at the 29 October pace, the only positive case in profit-model.md (low costs, N = 360) turns impossible once my time is worth more than **$12.63 an hour**, even if every willing person buys. At $20 an hour, its best price loses $884.

**And the volume isn't there.** At $51 and low costs, break-even is 106 buyers ($4,525 ÷ $42.64, matching the 107 in my Week 6 scale.md). At about an hour per willing person, that is roughly 106 hours on site, or about 18 mornings like 29 October, each needing the coach's introduction and a contest crowd.

So the margin survives the cost of reaching the person who pays it only if my time is nearly free, nearly every willing person buys, and I can recreate my one good morning about 18 times in a year. Reaching customers, not their willingness to pay, is what my funnel says is expensive in this business.
