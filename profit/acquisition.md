# What a customer costs

No money changed hands in any channel. Everything below is effort: approaches, hours, and a favor from the coach.

## Effort per response, by channel

| Channel | Approached | Finished | Approaches per finished response | My time | Money |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Torrance, Tue 29 Sep. Asked, then texted the link to finish later | 10 | 0 | no response from 10 | about 30 minutes | $0 |
| Luke Wells, Cal Poly SLO group chat. Posted link | ~50 | 1 | ~50 | one message to Luke. His time not counted | $0 |
| Torrance, Thu 29 Oct. Coach emailed parents first; most finished on the spot | 35 | 18 | **1.9** | **about 1.5 hours on site**, from the first response just before 9:00 to about 10:30 am. That is about 5 minutes per finished response | $0 |
| Personal network (sibling, cousin) | 2 | 2 | 1 | none | $0. Not a channel I can repeat |

**A caveat on the 5 minutes.** Responses from that day kept arriving until about 2:45 pm, after I had left. Some of the 18 probably came from parents using the coach's email link later. So part of that morning's yield was the coach's effort, not mine, and 5 minutes is a floor on what one response costs me without the coach's help.

The best channel worked because of two things I did not pay for and cannot buy more of: the coach vouching for me by email, and a contest crowd that stayed for five heats and food. Without them, the same lot produced nothing (29 September) and a warm introduction through a friend produced 2% (Luke).

## From responses to customers, in the best channel

A finished survey is not a customer. Of the 18 people who finished on 29 October:

| Price | Willing at that price | Approaches per willing person | My time per willing person |
|---|---|---|---|
| $35 | 12 of 18 | 2.9 | about 7½ minutes |
| $45 | 8 of 18 | 4.4 | about 11 minutes |
| $51 | 6 of 18 | 5.8 | about 15 minutes |

These assume every willing person actually buys. Nobody paid anything, so I have no measure of how many would.

## Margin per unit against the effort

| | Low costs | High costs |
|---|---|---|
| Margin per unit at $51 (p − c) | $42.64 | $32.45 |
| Margin per unit at $35 | $27.11 | $17.01 |

**How many units does one customer have to buy?** One, and that's all they will buy. PORTALO is durable, demand is yes/no, and nobody buys a second in the year. So the margin on the first unit has to cover the effort of finding that customer, or nothing ever will.

**If I had to pay for it.** Suppose I paid someone $20 an hour to do what I did on 29 October. That rate is my own round figure, not a measured cost. One willing person at $51 would then cost about **$5**, and at $35 about **$2.50**. If only half of willing people actually buy, those double to $10 and $5.

## Does the margin survive?

**Per customer, yes, under 29 October conditions.** At $51, a $5 cost of reaching a willing person is covered if at least 12% of willing people actually buy at low costs, or 15% at high costs.

**Across the whole commitment, only in the best case.** With effort counted at $20 an hour at the 29 October pace, the best case in profit-model.md (low costs, N = 360) still clears zero, but its peak falls from $1,516 to $916 and its band narrows from 23.8% to 15.8%. It turns impossible only if my time is worth more than about **$50 an hour**. In every other case it was already impossible before acquisition was counted.

**What doesn't survive is volume.** At $51 and low costs, break-even is 106 buyers ($4,525 ÷ $42.64, matching the 107 in my Week 6 scale.md). One morning like 29 October gave me 6 people willing at $51, so break-even takes about **18 mornings like it**, each one needing the coach's introduction and a contest crowd. On my other two channels, the same number of buyers would take thousands of approaches.

So reaching one willing person is cheap when the coach has vouched for me and the crowd is there. The expensive part is that those conditions are rare and not mine to create, and the volume I need depends on them happening about 18 times in a year.
